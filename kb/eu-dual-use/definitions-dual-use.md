## Key Definitions (reconstructed from Wassenaar Arrangement standard)

"Accuracy": Usually measured in terms of inaccuracy. It is the maximum deviation, positive or negative, of an indicated value from an accepted standard or true value.

"Active flight control systems": Systems functioning to prevent undesirable aircraft and missile motions or structural loads by autonomously processing outputs from multiple sensors.

"Adjusted Peak Performance" ("APP"): See Technical Note in Category 4.

"Aircraft": A fixed wing, swivel wing, rotary wing (helicopter), tilt rotor or tilt wing airborne vehicle.

"All compensations available": When applicable for a machine tool, all compensations normally provided by the manufacturer.

"Asymmetric algorithm": A cryptographic algorithm using different, mathematically related, keys for encryption and decryption.

"Authentication": The verification of the identity of a sender or receiver, or the verification of the integrity of data, messages, or other information.

"Basic scientific research": Experimental or theoretical work undertaken principally to acquire new knowledge of the fundamental principles of phenomena or observable facts, not primarily directed towards a specific practical aim or objective.

"Bias" (accelerometer): The output of an accelerometer that is not correlated with the input acceleration.

"Bias" (gyro): The output of a gyro that is not correlated with input rotation.

"CEP" (Circular Error Probable): In a circular normal distribution, the radius of the circle containing 50 percent of the individual measurements being made, or the radius of the circle within which there is a 50 percent probability of being located.

"Civil aircraft": Those aircraft listed by designation in published airworthiness certification lists by civil aviation authorities to fly commercial civil internal and external routes or for legitimate civil, private or business use.

"Circulation controlled anti-torque or circulation controlled direction control systems": Systems using air blown over aerodynamic surfaces to increase or control the forces generated by the surfaces.

"Composite": A "matrix" and an additional phase or additional phases consisting of fibres, whiskers, or particles.

"Contouring control": Two or more "numerically controlled" motions operating in accordance with instructions that specify the next required position and the required feed rates to that position. These feed rates are varied in relation to each other so that a desired contour is generated.

"Critical temperature": The "critical temperature" (sometimes referred to as the transition temperature) of a specific "superconductive" material is the temperature at which the material loses all resistance to the flow of direct electrical current.

"Cryptographic activation": Any technique that specifically activates or enables cryptographic capability of an item.

"Cryptography": The discipline which embodies principles, means and methods for the transformation of data in order to hide its information content, prevent its undetected modification or prevent its unauthorized use.

"CW laser": A "laser" that produces a nominally constant output energy for greater than 0.25 seconds.

"Data-Based Referenced Navigation" ("DBRN"): Systems which use any source of previously measured geo-mapping data in composite data-base referencing to provide accurate navigation data under dynamic conditions.

"Development": Related to all stages prior to serial production, such as design, design research, design analyses, design concepts, assembly and testing of prototypes, pilot production schemes, design data, process of transforming design data into a product, configuration design, integration design, layouts.

"Diffusion bonding": A molecular bonding of at least two separate metals into a joint with a joint strength equivalent to that of the weakest material.

"End-effectors": Grippers, "active tooling units" and any other tooling that is attached to the baseplate on the end of a "robot" manipulator arm.

"Equivalent density": The mass of an optic per unit of optical area projected onto the optical surface.

"Fibrous or filamentary materials": Includes continuous monofilaments, continuous yarns and rovings, tapes, fabrics, random mats and braids, chopped fibres, staple fibres and coherent fibre blankets, whiskers.

"Fly-by-light system": A primary digital flight control system employing feedback sensors, whose effector commands are optical signals.

"Fly-by-wire system": A primary digital flight control system employing feedback sensors, whose effector commands are electrical signals.

"Focal plane array": A linear or two-dimensional planar layer, or combination of planar layers, of individual detector elements, with or without readout electronics, which work in the focal plane.

"Frequency hopping": A form of "spread spectrum" in which the transmission frequency of a single communication channel is made to change by a random or pseudo-random sequence of discrete steps.

"Frequency switching time": The maximum time it takes a signal, when switched between any two random frequencies, to achieve the specified frequency tolerance.

"Guidance set": Systems that integrate the process of measuring and computing a vehicle's position and velocity with computing and issuing commands to the vehicle flight control systems to correct the trajectory.

"In the public domain": "Technology" or "software" which has been made available without restrictions upon its further dissemination. Note: Copyright restrictions do not remove "technology" or "software" from being "in the public domain".

"Information security": All the means and functions ensuring the accessibility, confidentiality or integrity of information or communications, excluding the means and functions intended to safeguard against malfunctions.

"Intrusion software": "Software" specially designed or modified to avoid detection by 'monitoring tools', or to defeat 'protective countermeasures', of a computer or network-capable device.

"Isostatic presses": Equipment capable of pressurising a closed cavity through various media to create equal pressure in all directions within the cavity upon a workpiece or material.

"Laser": An assembly of components which produce both spatially and temporally coherent light that is amplified by stimulated emission of radiation.

"Local area network": A data communication system having all of the following: allows an arbitrary number of independent 'data devices' to communicate directly with each other; and is confined to a geographical area of moderate size.

"Matrix": A substantially continuous phase that fills the space between particles, whiskers, or fibres.

"Missile": A complete rocket system or unmanned aerial vehicle system capable of a range exceeding 300 km.

"Monolithic integrated circuit": A combination of passive or active 'circuit elements' or both which are formed by means of diffusion processes, implantation processes, or deposition processes in or on a single semiconductor piece of material.

"Nuclear reactor": Includes the items within or attached directly to the reactor vessel, the equipment which controls the level of power in the core, and the components which normally contain or come in direct contact with or control the primary coolant of the reactor core.

"Numerical control": The automatic control of a process performed by a device that makes use of numeric data usually introduced as the operation is in progress.

"Production": Means all production stages, such as product engineering, manufacture, integration, assembly (mounting), inspection, testing, quality assurance.

"Programme": A sequence of instructions to carry out a process in, or convertible into, a form executable by an electronic computer.

"Pulsed laser": A "laser" having a "pulse duration" that is less than or equal to 0.25 seconds.

"Radiant sensitivity": A detection sensitivity measured in terms of current-per-watt incident radiant power (A/W).

"Required": As applied to "technology" or "software", refers to only that portion of "technology" or "software" which is peculiarly responsible for achieving or exceeding the controlled performance levels, characteristics or functions.

"Robot": A manipulation mechanism which may be of the continuous path or of the point-to-point type, may use sensors, and has all the following: is multifunctional; is capable of positioning or orienting material, parts, tools, or special devices through variable movements in three-dimensional space; incorporates three or more closed or open loop servo-devices; has user-accessible programmability.

"Satellite navigation system": A system that consists of ground stations, a constellation of satellites and user receivers, and provides calculated position from the satellites' transmitted signals.

"Signal processing": The processing of externally derived information-bearing signals by algorithms such as time compression, filtering, extraction, selection, correlation, convolution or transformations between domains.

"Software": A collection of one or more "programmes" or "microprogrammes" fixed in any tangible medium of expression.

"Source code": A convenient expression of one or more processes which may be turned by a programming system into equipment executable form ("object code" or object language).

"Spacecraft": Active and passive satellites and space probes.

"Spacecraft bus": Equipment that provides the physical structure, power, thermal control, pointing, propulsion and tracking/telemetry/command infrastructure for a "spacecraft payload".

"Spacecraft payload": Equipment, attached to the "spacecraft bus", designed to perform a mission in space.

"Specific modulus": Young's modulus in pascals, divided by specific weight in N/m3.

"Specific tensile strength": Ultimate tensile strength in pascals, divided by specific weight in N/m3.

"Spread spectrum": The technique whereby energy in a relatively narrowband communication channel is spread over a much wider energy spectrum.

"Sub-orbital craft": "Aircraft" designed to operate below orbital velocity, accelerated to flight by a propulsion system, including rocket-powered craft and those relying on other vehicles or aircraft for launch.

"Superalloy": Nickel-, cobalt-, or iron-base alloys having strengths superior to any alloy in the AISI 300 series at temperatures above 922K (649°C) under severe environmental conditions and operating times.

"Superconductive": Materials (i.e. metals, alloys or compounds) which can lose all electrical resistance (i.e. which can attain infinite electrical conductivity and carry very large electrical currents without Joule heating).

"Symmetric algorithm": A cryptographic algorithm using the same key for both encryption and decryption.

"Technology": Specific information necessary for the "development", "production" or "use" of a product. The information takes the form of "technical data" or "technical assistance".

"Tip shroud": A stationary ring component (solid or segmented) attached to the inner surface of the engine turbine casing or a component attached to the blade tip designed to provide a gas seal between the stationary and rotating components.

"Total control of flight": Automated control of the "aircraft" state variables and flight path to meet mission objectives responding to real time changes in data regarding objectives, hazards, or other "aircraft".

"Unidirectional positioning repeatability": The smallest R value (or R value for back, whichever is less) of any individual axis, defined by ISO 230-2:2014.

"Use": Operation, installation (including on-site installation), maintenance (checking), repair, overhaul and refurbishing.

"Vulnerability disclosure": The process of identifying, reporting, or communicating a vulnerability to, or analysing a vulnerability with, individuals or organizations responsible for conducting or coordinating remediation.
